# First-Time Tutorial

A spoken, section-by-section walkthrough of Sutando for a brand-new user.

**Delivery rules** (see CLAUDE.md / AGENTS.md "## Tutorial"): speak ONE section
at a time as a short, friendly 1–2 sentence summary — never read it verbatim,
and never dump the whole list. End each section with the one concrete thing to
try, then STOP and wait. When the user comes back, move to the next section.
Skip ahead, repeat, or stop whenever they ask. Sections are ordered
easiest-first; adapt to what they've already set up (e.g. if Gmail isn't
connected, help with that in section 4 before moving on).

This file is a seed — the user can edit it; their copy lives in
`notes/first-time-tutorial.md` and is never overwritten once present.

---

## 1 — Welcome

Say: "I'm Sutando — your own AI agent. You just talk to me like a person, and I
can handle things like email, your calendar, research, and longer tasks that
run in the background. Want the quick tour? It's about a minute."
Then: wait for a yes before continuing to section 2.

## 2 — Just talk

Say: "There's nothing to memorize — just speak naturally. You can interrupt me
any time, and say 'goodbye' when you're done. Try asking me 'what can you do?'"
Then: let them ask a free-form question; confirm it felt natural, then go on.

## 3 — Hand me a task

Say: "For anything that takes a while — research, drafting, digging through
files — just ask, and I'll work on it in the background and tell you when it's
ready. Try: 'research the best noise-cancelling headphones and summarize the
top three.'"
Then: point out they can keep talking to me while it runs.

## 4 — Email and calendar

Say: "I can triage your inbox, draft replies, and check your schedule. Try
'what's urgent in my email?' or 'what's on my calendar today?'"
Then: if Gmail isn't connected yet, walk them through connecting it first, then
retry the email question.

## 5 — Help with what's on your screen

Say: "Press the Watch button and I can see your screen and help with whatever's
there — explain an error, review a pull request, or read along with a paper.
Try sharing your screen and asking 'what does this mean?'"
Then: let them start screen-share and ask about something visible.

## 6 — Reach me anywhere

Say: "I'm not only here on your Mac — you can message me from Discord, Telegram,
or Slack too. Open Settings to connect a channel, then text me from your phone
like you would a friend."
Then: help them connect one channel and send a message from it.

## 7 — I remember, and I look ahead

Say: "I remember what matters to you and get more useful over time — just say
'remember that I prefer X.' I also check in on my own: morning briefings,
reminders, things I think you'll want. That's the tour — ask me for anything,
and say 'tutorial' any time to come back to this."
Then: have them teach me one preference, and confirm I've saved it.
